  1. THE CHECKLIST, ordered by what disqualifies a deployment first

     (a) N_eff > 2        does a (cost centre, document type) cell hold more
                          than two candidate accounts? If a cell names the
                          account outright, no aliasing of the account code can
                          help and LC-FPE does not apply. Binary gate; measure
                          it on the real chart before anything else.
     (b) R <= alpha - m   partition size. The account field width alpha is
                          rarely negotiable, so this fixes how often the books
                          must be partitioned.
     (c) m <= ~400        chart size, measured. Beyond that recovery grows and
                          we have not measured the boundary.
     (d) n >= eq(histsize)  history for f_hat. The only constraint that cannot
                          be fixed by configuration -- only by waiting, or by
                          accepting the larger Delta.

  3. THE TENSION
     Partitions per year rises with firm size; years of history needed falls.
     The two cross, and where they cross is the only comfortable operating point.

        rows/yr   parts/yr  yrs hist
          3,000          1     23.07
         10,000          2      6.92
         30,000          4      2.31
        100,000         11      0.69
        300,000         31      0.23
      1,000,000        104      0.07

     At m=300, alpha=10^4: K = 9,700, S = 10.2, so a clean f_hat needs 69206 rows.
     A firm reaches that in one year only once it posts ~69,206 rows a year --- by
     which point it already needs 8 partitions. There is no firm size at which
     both constraints are slack at once, and a deployer must choose which to relax.

     Note the 5-digit row above. Widening the account field is the obvious way to
     cut partitions -- 13 down to 2 -- but n_req grows as K^2, so the history needed
     goes from 0.6 years to 61. A wider field buys a tighter allocation bound m/2K,
     and f_hat must then be estimated well enough to keep up with it. Widening the
     field without the history to match makes Delta worse, not better.

  4. OUR OWN BENCHMARK, CHECKED AGAINST THE RULE
     tab:base measures a 20,000-row ledger at m=200, K' = 9,800.
     occupancy = 2.04, which is ABOVE the design rule R/K' <= 1.
     That is deliberate and it is not a security configuration: encryption cost
     is per row and independent of the partition boundary, so the throughput
     figure is unaffected. A compliant deployment splits those rows into 3
     partitions, which changes rows/s not at all and adds two more anchors.
